Game Design Principles Research

May 28, 2026

As part of this week's readings, I read the article titled Game Design Principles written by Sabqat Ruba and posted on Juego Studios. In it, there are several key design principles of video games outlined, among which two principles struck me personally because of my experience both as a player and a developing game designer, namely the principles "Engaging Core Mechanics" and "Game Flow."

Super Mario is used to illustrate how the game can be based on simple mechanics such as jumping and running. This relates to me personally, since I realize that the games I really enjoy do not necessarily have complicated control schemes, but only perfect implementation of simple maneuvers. If a game requires me to focus on my controller, or even struggle with the confusing interface of the game, then the very flow of the game will be ruined, since all actions should feel mindless and effortless.

Applying this concept to our current design projects, I see that increasing features does not necessarily have to be the solution. Rather, achieving a balance in terms of complexity and providing hints to the next step to be performed by the gamer—such as the treasure chest and the lock in the example provided in the text—can be the key to a smooth user experience. Going forward, it will be my aim to keep our game controls easy to understand and follow.

Visual Thinking Analysis

May 7, 2026

Part One: Peer's Image Analysis

The picture that will be discussed presents an idea of personal belongings through depicting their contents including books, other everyday items, and glasses. Concerning the structure, all the objects in the picture are presented on the basis of transparent backgrounds in order to separate them visually as unique parts forming the whole picture. As far as the general atmosphere goes, the entire scene is imbued with yellow tones creating the feeling of nostalgia and comfort. Therefore, in terms of the visual narrative, the picture successfully conveys both the evident feature of things and the mystery of their choice by the person carrying them.

In order to expand the picture in the direction away from documentary presentation, it would be interesting to highlight some particular features of each object thus enabling people to make clear distinctions. For instance, it is possible to show an opened tube of lipstick with its color and texture. Moreover, the addition of another picture, where it would be shown how these things are used, would turn the picture into an absolutely fascinating visual narrative.

Part Two: Personal Image Analysis

The chosen photograph for this study captures some of the practice artworks done by me in the period when I was studying art as a major. The interesting aspect of these works that is not necessarily seen in the photograph is the background and context of their creation. It should be noted that the images featured in the picture are not final products but are the practice sketches that were produced during my intensive training sessions as part of my studies. In other words, one might only notice some simple sketches but not the real essence that can be captured through dedication and hard work.

It should be mentioned that the chosen picture is closely related to the central idea of my archive. As mentioned earlier, the theme of my archives is about the journey of a complete beginner in learning art through rigorous practices. Thus, my archive reflects my personal story of discipline and progress in art. At the same time, one can make the image much more appealing to the user by changing the pictures and making interactive illustrations.

Overuse of Overlays Research

April 23, 2026

The following information was obtained from an article by the Nielsen Norman Group titled “Overuse of Overlays.” Lightboxes have been studied on their effect on usability. Although overlays are beneficial to provide context, they could be detrimental if they are not properly used. It is important to use the “Five W’s” to determine whether an overlay is appropriate.

The observations concerning mobile accessibility were quite enlightening to me. Overlays that hide certain content or do not offer an easy way out could prove catastrophic to accomplishing tasks. This means that, when designing, I need to include "close" instructions that are clearly visible and that are also reachable using the Escape button. To learn more, the approach used by Apple from using overlays to dedicated pages is exemplary.

Form Design Best Practices

April 14, 2026

“Best Practices For Form Design” is highlighted that the main aim of any form on the web should be to achieve conversions. The most interesting idea discussed in the article is related to the principle of lowering the level of cognitive burden on users. This could be achieved through limiting the number of fields and employing one-column designs.

Additionally, I have understood the significance of "affordance" using the technique of field sizing. Making the width of the field the same size as the amount of data to be entered acts as a visual indicator for the user. The article clearly illustrates that making web pages accessible should not be considered an add-on; instead, it is a basic responsibility.
